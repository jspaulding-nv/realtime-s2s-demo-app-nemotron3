2. Complete an independent first pass

Fixed landmark rule

Mark the first PCM sample at the onset of the source word that completes the idea, then mark the first PCM sample at the onset of the corresponding translated word.

  1. Work independently and keep this first pass blind until its observation has been downloaded.
  2. Judge the meaning and audible boundary yourself. Do not substitute a pause, frame edge, schedule event, or convenient timestamp.
  3. The source marker must stay inside its assigned source window. The translated marker must identify the corresponding landmark, wherever it occurs in the translated recording.
  4. Use uncertain or rejected when semantic correspondence is unclear; never force acceptance.
  5. If the translated speech omits the idea and no corresponding word onset exists, mark the nearest audible candidate or the surrounding phrase where it was expected. Choose rejected, flag omission (and boundary ambiguous when applicable), and set translated-boundary confidence to 1. That location is diagnostic only and cannot support reconciliation.

4. Review every assigned event

Select an event to highlight its source window. Set each marker from the matching playhead, or enter an exact sample and use the nudge buttons.

For every boundary-confidence and listening-quality scale, 1 is lowest and 5 is highest.

Shared 1/3/5 rating anchors
  • Boundary confidence: 1 = guess; 3 = identifiable with some onset ambiguity; 5 = precise, repeatable onset.
  • Translation quality: 1 = meaning is wrong or substantially missing; 3 = core meaning is understandable with notable flaws; 5 = meaning is fully preserved.
  • Intelligibility: 1 = difficult to understand; 3 = generally understandable with effort; 5 = immediately clear.
  • Naturalness: 1 = strongly unnatural; 3 = noticeably synthetic or uneven; 5 = natural and appropriate.

Use 2 or 4 when the judgment falls between anchors.
